WORKER HEAT SAFETY

Heat kills.
Heat illness can be prevented.

Heat cramps, fainting, heat exhaustion, and heatstroke can cause serious illness or death. Know the symptoms and make a plan before the shift.

Learn the warning signs ↓For outdoor workers and crews
!

Get medical help! If you feel symptoms, tell your coworkers and supervisor immediately. Call 911 for life-threatening symptoms.

KNOW THE SYMPTOMS

Watch for yourself and your coworkers.

Before each workday, know who to talk to and how to get help. Early action can prevent a serious emergency.

EARLY SYMPTOMS

Act early.

↗

Tell coworkers and your supervisor right away. You may need medical help.

  • Fatigue
  • Heavy sweating
  • Headache
  • Cramps
  • Dizziness
  • High pulse rate
  • Nausea or vomiting

LIFE-THREATENING

Call 911 now.

!

These signs require immediate medical help. Stay with the person and cool them while help is coming.

  • High body temperature
  • Red, hot, dry skin
  • Confusion
  • Convulsions
  • Fainting

Know how to get help.

Tell someone before you start work who your supervisor is and where to call for emergency service.

PREVENTING HEAT ILLNESS

Build safety into the whole shift.

Use these habits every day, especially during a heat wave or when someone is new to working in the heat.

01

Tell your supervisor

  • Speak up if you are new to working in the heat.
  • Tell them if you have had heat illness before.
02

Stay alert to the weather

  • During a heat wave, watch yourself and coworkers more closely.
  • Plan for more water, more breaks, and other protective measures.
03

Drink cool, fresh water

  • Drink at least one 8-ounce cup every 15 minutes throughout your shift.
  • Do not wait until you are thirsty. Choose water over soft drinks; avoid alcohol and coffee.
04

Rest in the shade

  • Take rest breaks in the shade to cool down.
  • Use a cool spot or air conditioning when available.
05

Wear proper clothing

  • Choose loose-fitting, lightweight, light-colored cotton clothing.
  • Wear a wide-brimmed hat or cap and a bandana.
06

Talk to your doctor

  • Ask about heat risk if you have an illness such as diabetes.
  • Ask if medicines or a low-salt diet affect your heat safety.

PLAN THE DAY

A safer shift starts before the heat does.

Use the forecast, pace the work, and build in ways to cool down. This is your quick heat-safety reset.

⌁01

Check the forecast

Know when the heat will peak so you can slow down or move strenuous work to a cooler time.

◆02

Drink before thirst

Keep water available and take regular sips. Don’t wait until you feel thirsty.

◒03

Slow down & cool

Take breaks in shade or air conditioning. Use the coolest part of the day for hard work.

♧04

Check on others

Pay attention to children, older adults, coworkers, and anyone who may need extra help in the heat.

CALIFORNIA OUTDOOR WORKERS

Know your rights.

If you work outdoors in California, your employer must provide basic heat-illness protections.

◆

Cool water

Enough cool, fresh drinking water throughout the day.

◒

Shade & rest

Access to shade or an equally cool spot for at least 5 minutes at a time.

✚

Training

Training on prevention and how to call emergency services.

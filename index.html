BEAT THE HEAT

Four habits that protect the whole crew

Heat stress happens when your body stores more heat than it can release. Make cooling part of the work plan.

01💧

Water, early & often

Drink about 1 cup (8 oz.) every 15–20 minutes during moderate work in the heat. Keep cool water close by.

02◒

Rest in shade

Take frequent breaks in a shaded or air-conditioned area. As heat, humidity, workload, or PPE increase, rest more.

03♧

Use the buddy system

Check on coworkers often. Ask two simple questions. If they hesitate more than 3 seconds or answer strangely, stop work.

04✦

Wear for the weather

Choose loose, lightweight, breathable, light-colored clothing. Protective clothing and PPE can add heat burden.

NEW TO HOT WORK?

Build up slowly.
Give your body time.

Acclimatization means gradually getting used to heat. New workers should start at no more than 20% of the usual heat exposure on day 1, then increase by no more than 20% each day over 7–14 days.

NOTICE THE CHANGE

Know the warning signs

Heat illness can get worse quickly. Stop, cool down, and get help when something feels wrong.

ACT EARLY↘

Heat exhaustion

Headache · nausea · dizziness · weakness · irritability · thirst · heavy sweating

Move to a cool area. Sip cool water. Get medical evaluation.
ACT NOW!

Heat stroke = emergency

Confusion · altered mental status · slurred speech · seizures · loss of consciousness · very high body temperature

Call 911. Stay with the person. Move them to shade and cool them quickly.
